rshow: a link that keeps working

Publishing, versions, sharing and caching.

Dana Ruiz, rshow project, weekly research meeting, 14 March

Say who you are and what the room gets out of this: one command, one link that stays put, no build step to babysit.

Promise: by the end they can publish a deck, a report or a demo from a folder on their laptop.

Every publish is a version, and a version never changes.

The whole talk sits in that sentence. Everything after it is a consequence, so give it a moment before moving on.

Four parts make a link survive an edit

  • A publish writes a numbered version and leaves the old ones alone.
  • A pin holds one version in place while the next one is half written.
  • A share decides who may read the link: private, people or public.
  • A content hash means a republish uploads only what changed.
  • The slug stays the same, so the link in the calendar invite still works.

Advance one line at a time. Pause after the first: immutability is the part people do not expect.

A pin freezes one version, the newest link keeps moving

Pinned

  • One version, chosen by you.
  • The link in the invite keeps showing it.
  • You publish behind it until the talk is ready.

Newest

  • Whatever was published last.
  • Good for a page you keep editing.
  • Every publish moves it, with no warning.

Ask the room which one they would send to a reviewer. Most people want the pin, and then admit they never set one.

Publish the folder, then keep the link

rshow publish ./my-talk \
  --slug my-talk \
  --label "first draft"

The label is what changed. It is the one line a reader uses to pick a version.

Run the command live if the network allows it, then open the printed link. The token comes from the environment, never from this line.
